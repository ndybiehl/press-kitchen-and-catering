About Us

Welcome Home, Chef James Schendel

There is nothing quite like the flavor of home—especially when that home is Billings. After a culinary journey across the Northwest, Chef James Schendel is finally back where it all began. A Montana culinary school graduate with a background in high-end catering and restaurant management, James most recently spent a standout stint as a brewery chef in Oregon. While the Pacific Northwest’s craft scene was incredible, the pull of the “406” and the dream of cooking on his own terms brought him back to his roots.

Trading the brewery grind for the open road, James—alongside his partner in crime, Muriah, and their crew of animals—packed up and headed for Big Sky Country. Now, while James is in the kitchen serving up his signature gourmet flavors, Muriah is holding down the front of the house, ensuring the service is just as good as the food.

Catch the team bringing a fresh, bold taste and a friendly face to the streets of Billings. From culinary school to the brewery and back again, this isn’t just a business launch—it’s a homecoming.

Catering

Bring people together

At Press Kitchen & Catering, we believe great food brings people together. Whether you’re planning a wedding, corporate luncheon, holiday party, private celebration, fundraiser, or community event, we’re committed to creating a memorable dining experience from start to finish.

With over 20 years of combined hospitality and catering experience, our team specializes in fresh, handcrafted menus tailored to your vision, budget, and event style. From elegant buffets and grazing tables to hors d’oeuvres, brunches, and full-service meals, every menu is thoughtfully prepared using high-quality ingredients and exceptional attention to detail.

We pride ourselves on outstanding customer service, professional presentation, and delicious food that your guests will remember long after the event is over. No matter the size of your gathering, we’re passionate about making your event stress-free, seamless, and unforgettable.
